Lingua Franca

Today, work moves between crafts by hand. Product writes a ticket, Design hands over a file, and Engineering asks in chat what was meant. Ticket quality varies from project to project. Each pair of crafts has its own way to talk, and every hand-over loses a little meaning.

Agents amplify this. They read and write as fast as people, or faster, but they cannot read between the lines. They need facts that are written down, in one place. And more importantly: with consistent naming. A teaser is a component is a module is an editorial is a … thing. How is an agent supposed to know what you're talking about when reading the ticket?

We need a common language, for humans and agents.

Lingua Franca is that language.

The Center

Each craft has its own language. Product talks in features and outcomes, Design in components and flows, Engineering in modules and APIs. Lingua Franca does not replace these languages. It places the crafts around one common Center, and each craft connects to it on its own. What must be shared lives in the Center. Everything else stays with the craft.

Every craft connects to the Center, never to each other.

The Center holds what all crafts share: the domain language, the history and reasoning behind decisions, common agreements like ticket and spec formats, DoR, DoD, and more. Each craft connects to it, and keeps its own tools and rules locally.

This page starts with Engineering, the first craft to connect. Read it in order:

  1. Principlesthe rules every part follows.
  2. Slots and Piecesneeds, and what fills them.
  3. Tierswhat is mandatory and what is optional.
  4. Layersthe four jobs a Piece can do.

You do not need to adopt everything at once. Start with the mandatory slots, then add what helps your project.

Principles

Before we look at the parts, here is what holds them together. Lingua Franca follows eight principles. They explain why each part exists, and why it looks the way it does.

Each principle has a short tag, like AA or DD. Later on the page, a tag next to a layer or slot tells you which principle it comes from.

AA

Agent-agnosticity

No lock-in to one agent tool.

WI

Workflow independence

No fixed way of working.

MO

Modularity

Adopt or skip each slot alone.

PD

Progressive disclosure

Small entry point, detail on demand.

DL

Deterministic language

One name per concept.

RP

Reverse prompting

The agent asks, the human thinks.

DD

Derivation over duplication

Each fact is written once. Every copy is derived.

EL

Enforcement Ladder

Push each rule as high as it goes:

  1. 1impossible (derived)
  2. 2deterministic check
  3. 3probabilistic check
  4. 4written only

Slots and Pieces

MO

Lingua Franca works like a jigsaw puzzle. The Center and each craft have holes: things a project needs, like a shared domain language or a way to test code. We call a hole a Slot. What fills it is a Piece.

Slot

A need. A place in the system that Pieces fill.

Piece

What fills the slot: a KB file, a skill, a check, a tool.

Layer

Where a Piece lives: Context (describe), Skills (prescribe), Guardrails (correct), Tools (connect).

Family

Where a slot lives, and who it serves. Center: shared by all crafts. Connection: for communication between crafts. Local: specific to one craft.

A slot usually needs more than one Piece to work, often spread across several layers. Lingua Franca ships sensible defaults for most of them, but you're free to swap any of them for something that suits your project better.

Tiers

Not every Slot is equally required. Some are the minimum the Center needs to stay readable, so every project has them. Others are optional, and you pick them when they help.

The tier also says who provides the Piece: Lingua Franca (LF), or you (BYO, bring your own).

We keep the mandatory part as small as we can: just what the Center needs to stay readable. Everything else is yours to pick, skip or reshape.

Layers

Every Piece does one of four jobs. It describes how things are, prescribes what to do, checks that it was done right, or connects to a system outside the repo. That job is the Piece's Layer.
